Water Supply Infrastructure in El Camino Village

Bordering Crenshaw Boulevard, Redondo Beach Boulevard, and adjacent to El Camino College, El Camino Village is a post-WWII residential master-planned subdivision featuring classic mid-century ranch architecture. Built primarily between 1946 and 1955, these residences were constructed with original galvanized steel main water service lines connected to street-side water meters. Having surpassed their 50-year design lifespan, these lines commonly exhibit severe internal scale, weak flow volume, and recurring underground pinhole leaks.

Common plumbing challenges faced by El Camino Village property owners:

Galvanized Pipe Aging & Low Pressure

In many El Camino Village residences, the original 3/4-inch galvanized supply line has narrowed significantly due to rust accumulation. This creates a severe restriction where running a washing machine or irrigation system causes showers to lose all pressure.

Upgrading through galvanized pipe replacement restores full water pressure throughout the property. Learn more about diagnosing flow bottlenecks with our low water pressure repair guide.

Protecting Poured Concrete Driveways

A signature feature of El Camino Village homes is the extended concrete driveway leading to rear garages. Conventional open-trench plumbing would destroy these paved surfaces. By using trenchless water line replacement, contractors bore directly beneath the driveway, leaving the concrete completely intact.

Frequently Asked Questions in El Camino Village

Why do homes in El Camino Village experience water service line leaks?

Most homes in El Camino Village were built in the late 1940s and 1950s with original galvanized steel plumbing. Decades of mineral exposure and shifting South Bay soil have caused these pipes to rust from the inside out, leading to pinhole leaks and flow restrictions.

Can a new water line be installed under my concrete driveway?

Yes. Using trenchless pipe-pulling or horizontal directional boring, contractors can install a seamless, continuous PEX-a or copper pipe under your driveway without cutting, jackhammering, or repaving the concrete.
